# Apple App Store review: public iOS app with organization-issued login

**Reviewed:** 2026-09-15
**Scope:** Official Apple documentation only; this is a product/review-readiness report, not legal advice.

## Findings

1. **Public App Store distribution does not, in the cited Apple distribution documentation, require public self-registration.** Apple defines Public Distribution as an app “publicly available on the App Store” in selected countries or regions. Apple separately describes Private Distribution for specific businesses/organizations and Unlisted Distribution for direct-link discovery. These are distribution choices; the cited documentation does not say that a publicly distributed app must offer public account creation. [Apple: Set distribution methods](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/manage-your-apps-availability/set-distribution-methods) (public/private/unlisted distinctions, lines 405–409, 424–453).

2. **An organization-issued login is expressly contemplated for business apps.** Apple’s Login Services guideline says the equivalent-login-service requirement does not apply when “your app is an education, enterprise, or business app that requires the user to sign in with an existing education or enterprise account.” It also exempts apps that exclusively use the company’s own account setup and sign-in systems. This supports GND Millwork using pre-provisioned company accounts without adding Sign in with Apple or another social-login alternative solely for App Review. [Apple: App Review Guidelines, 4.8 Login Services](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/) (lines 461–473).

3. **Apple’s separate account-sign-in rule is feature-sensitive.** If an app does not include significant account-based features, Apple says to let people use it without a login; it also says apps may not require personal information except when directly relevant to core functionality or required by law. For an account-centered business workflow, document in App Review notes why authenticated access is core functionality and why an organization-issued account is required. [Apple: App Review Guidelines, 5.1.1(v) Account Sign-In](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/) (lines 498–500).

4. **App Review must be able to exercise the authenticated app.** Apple asks developers to provide App Review “full access”; for account-based features, provide either an active demo account or a fully featured demo mode, plus required credentials/resources. Backend services must be live and accessible during review. The more specific submission guidance also says to include demo-account information when the app includes a login; if a demo account cannot be supplied for legal/security reasons, a built-in demo mode requires Apple’s prior approval. [Apple: App Review Guidelines, Before You Submit](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/) (lines 152–158); [Apple: App Review Guidelines, 2.1(a)](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/) (lines 237–240).

5. **In-app account deletion is required when the app supports account creation.** Apple’s deletion support page says apps submitted to the App Store that support account creation must let users initiate deletion within the app. The option should be easy to find, delete the entire account and associated data not legally required to retain, and may link directly to the precise web page if web completion is necessary. Temporarily disabling an account is insufficient. [Apple: Offering account deletion in your app](https://developer.apple.com/support/offering-account-deletion-in-your-app) (lines 130–140).

   For GND’s current model (existing active company accounts, with no in-app self-registration), the cited Apple requirement is worded around apps that **support account creation**; it does not state that every app that merely authenticates pre-provisioned accounts must add self-registration. If GND later adds account creation (including automatic/guest account creation), the deletion requirement applies; Apple explicitly says automatically generated accounts also need a deletion option. [Apple: Offering account deletion in your app](https://developer.apple.com/support/offering-account-deletion-in-your-app) (lines 151–159).

6. **Privacy policy placement is mandatory both in listing metadata and in the app.** Apple requires a privacy-policy link in App Store Connect metadata and inside the app in an easily accessible manner. The policy must cover data collected, collection/use, relevant third-party protections, retention/deletion, and how users can revoke consent or request deletion. App Store Connect separately requires an iOS privacy-policy URL and accurate App Privacy disclosures, including third-party partners’ practices. [Apple: App Review Guidelines, 5.1.1(i)](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/) (lines 486–494); [Apple: Manage app privacy](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/manage-app-information/manage-app-privacy) (lines 403–408, 469–486).

## Practical review checklist for GND Millwork

- Keep the public listing/distribution decision separate from the identity policy: public availability can coexist with an organization-issued login under Apple’s cited rules.
- Identify the app as a business workflow in metadata and App Review notes; explain that access requires an existing company account and that there is no public self-registration.
- Supply a review-only active account with full representative access, live backend, and any required setup data; never rely on reviewers being able to create an account.
- Add an easily reachable in-app privacy-policy link and complete App Store Connect App Privacy answers accurately.
- Add in-app account deletion if GND introduces account creation or automatic account provisioning. For the current pre-provisioned-account model, confirm the exact deletion/account-lifecycle design with counsel and Apple during review if needed.

No Apple document reviewed here says that a publicly discoverable App Store app must provide public sign-up. The conclusion above is limited to the cited Apple documentation and should not be expanded into a claim that Apple will approve every business-only login design.
