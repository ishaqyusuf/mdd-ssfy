### P1 - Generic public task triggering remains too powerful for operations

`apps/api/src/trpc/routers/task-trigger.route.ts:6` exposes `trigger: publicProcedure`, accepts `taskName: z.string()` and `payload: z.any()` at lines 9-10, calls `tasks.trigger(params.taskName, ...)` at line 15, and exposes run `output` / `error` in status at lines 45-46. This is the highest operational-control risk because it bypasses a typed, permissioned command model for background work that can affect inventory, sales, dispatch, jobs, or notifications.

### P1 - Public payment/device side-effect routes still need boundary separation

`apps/api/src/trpc/routers/checkout.route.ts:28`, `:33`, `:38`, and `:43` keep quote acceptance, sales checkout link creation, payment verification, and Square device-code generation as public procedures. Some public token flows are legitimate customer entry points, but `generateDeviceCode` is an operational device-management action and should not share the same trust posture as customer checkout. `apps/api/src/trpc/routers/square-test.route.ts:6` is also public and creates a Square terminal checkout at line 16 with a timestamp idempotency key at line 18.

Final dispatch completion is stronger: `dispatch-complete-form.tsx` preserves a request id, requires signature, and supports photo attachments, but that proof is after packing rather than at the packing decision point.

## Worker Usability Review

Production staff now have better in-context guardrails: Sales Overview production readiness and worker submission blocking make material availability visible before work is reported. This supports trainable behavior by preventing workers from pushing blocked production downstream.

Warehouse/mobile packing remains less robust. The app has a separate warehouse packing workspace and quantity validation, but manual fields are still easy to mis-key under shop-floor pressure. A scan-first flow with large item cards, barcode/QR confirmation, and optional photo proof would better match mixed technical comfort levels.

Office/admin users still face too many public-route and legacy state seams. Active Sessions mock data, generic task triggering, and broad public filters create a gap between what the UI appears to control and what the API actually enforces.

## Customer And Dealer Experience Review

Dealer next-step guidance is materially better than a raw status list. It distinguishes quote review, approval, GND payable, customer balance, ready states, and cancellation/refund contact states.

The customer/dealer gap is manufacturing transparency. For doors, customers and dealers often need a simple answer: "accepted, material covered, in production, ready for pickup, delivered." Current dealer guidance reaches ready-for-fulfillment, but it does not expose material/order coverage, production assignment, production blocker, or delivery scheduling proof.

Client-ready delivery status is already planned as documentation, but the product should eventually generate a customer/dealer-safe status view from actual evidence rather than relying on manual summaries after sessions.

## Door-Manufacturing Workflow Gaps

- Quote/order readiness should check door dimensions, swing/handing, no-handle, profile/tax/customer defaults, supplier price coverage, material/inbound coverage, production assignment, and shipping/pickup readiness as one plain-language checklist.
- Production readiness exists in dashboard context, but dealer and mobile surfaces do not consume the same compact readiness vocabulary.
- Inventory repairs remain paused, so stock/inbound/demand states cannot yet be treated as release-clean manufacturing truth.
- Packing and dispatch proof exists at completion, but item-level packing proof is not scan-first and does not naturally attach photos to ambiguous or high-risk items.
- Public task/payment/device routes make accountability hard: workers need clear "who did what" operational actions, not generic remote triggers.
