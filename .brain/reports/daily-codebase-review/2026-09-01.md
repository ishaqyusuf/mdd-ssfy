## Highest-Risk Findings

P1 - Public Square test checkout remains mounted and can create live Terminal checkout side effects.

Evidence: `apps/api/src/trpc/routers/square-test.route.ts:1` imports `publicProcedure`; `apps/api/src/trpc/routers/square-test.route.ts:6` exposes `test`; `apps/api/src/trpc/routers/square-test.route.ts:16` calls `squareClient.terminal.checkouts.create`; `apps/api/src/trpc/routers/_app.ts:97` mounts `squareTest`.

Why it matters: this bypasses the safer staff terminal-payment contract documented in `.brain/api/contracts.md`, including device re-query, paired-device intersection, readiness `PING`, and local pending-row sequencing. In door-manufacturing operations, accidental or unauthorized checkout prompts erode counter-staff trust and can confuse real terminal users.

P1 - Public scheduled task-event controls remain available for list/update/run operations.

Evidence: `apps/api/src/trpc/routers/task-events.route.ts:10` imports `publicProcedure`; `apps/api/src/trpc/routers/task-events.route.ts:18` creates the router; `update`, `runNow`, `runTest`, and `runStatus` are public at lines 31, 53, 58, and 67; `apps/api/src/trpc/routers/_app.ts:100` mounts `taskEvents`.

Why it matters: task-event status and run controls influence background automation behavior. Even if individual task handlers have internal checks, the scheduler control plane should not be callable through a public tRPC boundary by anonymous, customer, or dealer contexts.

P1 - Public money/customer routes still expose broad payment context and side effects.

Evidence: `apps/api/src/trpc/routers/checkout.route.ts:18` through `43` expose checkout initialization, quote acceptance, payment-link creation, payment verification, and device-code generation as public procedures. `apps/api/src/trpc/routers/customer.route.ts:58` exposes `getCustomerPayPortal` publicly; `apps/api/src/db/queries/customer.ts:2064` accepts only `accountNo`, then reads pending sales and wallet state at lines 2077-2079. `apps/api/src/trpc/routers/sales.route.ts:1439`, `1451`, `1456`, `1461`, `1488`, `1494`, `1502`, `1507`, `1512`, and `1517` show additional public sales/payment/supplier/accounting mutations or reads.

Why it matters: some public checkout/quote procedures are intentionally tokenized customer flows, but the broad public grouping still mixes quote acceptance, payment creation, payment verification, account-number pay portal lookup, wallet context, supplier/accounting access, and sales mutation behavior. This is risky around revenue, customer trust, and dealership/customer separation.

P1 - Public organization create/list remains inconsistent with office and future tenant scoping.

Evidence: `apps/api/src/db/queries/organization.ts:1` imports `publicProcedure`; `getOrganizationProfile` reads all organizations at line 9; `createOrganizationProfile` is public at line 27 and creates an organization at lines 34-41.

Why it matters: Brain plans treat Organization as office/location authority and explicitly separate it from future SaaS Tenant authority. Public organization writes are incompatible with office-scoped accountability, fulfillment-office assignment, and multi-location auditability.

P2 - Broad public filter/read metadata remains available for operational surfaces.

Evidence: `apps/api/src/trpc/routers/filters.route.ts:43` through `66` expose customer, customer service, dispatch, employee, inbound, builder, inventory, and job filters as public; lines 91-117 expose sales order/quote/accounting filters as public. `apps/api/src/trpc/routers/sales.route.ts:977` exposes `getOrders` publicly and line 980 exposes `getOrdersSummary` publicly.

Why it matters: filter endpoints often leak real operational names, employees, customers, jobs, inventory categories, and work states. Even when they are only metadata, they shape what a non-employee can infer about production volume, customer activity, warehouse state, and sales pipeline.

P2 - Monorepo typecheck remains red at a shared package boundary.

Evidence: `packages/errors/package.json:5` declares ESM; `packages/errors/src/index.ts:1-21` uses extensionless relative exports; `packages/settings/tsconfig.json:6-7` opts into NodeNext. The broad typecheck fails before the full workspace can be trusted.

Why it matters: this blocks broad validation of revenue, dispatch, dealership, mobile, and inventory changes. It also keeps unrelated future work from distinguishing new failures from baseline noise.

P2 - Mobile packing quantity drafts are still volatile compared with proof drafts.

Evidence: `apps/mobile/src/features/dispatch/components/dispatch-detail-screen/hooks/use-packing-slip-drafts.ts:32` keeps packing quantities in React state and line 36 reinitializes them when the sheet opens. In contrast, proof drafts have AsyncStorage and document-directory persistence in `apps/mobile/src/features/dispatch/lib/dispatch-proof-draft-storage.ts:36`, `40`, `85`, and `123`.

Why it matters: warehouse workers and drivers can recover signature/photo proof after interruption, but count-entry work can still disappear before submission. For mixed-skill workers doing door counts, LH/RH quantities, and packed-item checks under weak network conditions, losing partial count work causes rework and mistakes.

P2 - Dealer next-step guidance is still payment/fulfillment coarse and not manufacturing-readiness specific.

Evidence: `apps/dealership/src/lib/dealer-next-step.ts:107-178` derives next steps from GND balance, customer balance, delivery option, order status, and bounded fulfillment status. It does not expose dimensions, swing/handing, material blockers, production readiness, packing proof, or ETA evidence.

Why it matters: dealer users can see preparing or ready, but not why. Door operations need clarity around what is waiting on pricing review, material allocation, production, packing, pickup, delivery scheduling, or customer balance.

P3 - Active Sessions remains a mock UI while auth/session tracking exists elsewhere.

Evidence: `apps/dashboard/src/components/user-logged-in-devices.tsx:9` defines `mockDevices`; line 38 keeps logout as TODO; line 48 renders mock data. Auth packages now record Better Auth session/device metadata, so the settings UI no longer matches platform capability.

Why it matters: account security and device accountability matter for office/admin staff, dealers, and shared terminals. A mock session list teaches users that session controls exist when they do not actually revoke devices.

## Worker Usability Review

- Sales reps benefit from recent New Sales Form due-date and save-path cleanup, but the public route surface still makes it hard to reason about which money/customer actions are internal staff actions versus customer-token actions.
- Production workers now have stronger assignment/submission visibility and permission locks, but the broad typecheck baseline keeps release confidence lower than it should be for production-payroll and material-review work.
- Warehouse/driver mobile work is much better than older reviews: direct dispatch packing routes are protected, `confirmPacking` rechecks assignment/manager/packing authority, and proof drafts are recoverable. The remaining usability risk is interruption recovery for pre-submit packing quantities.
- Managers still need simple oversight: unresolved public task-event controls and broad filters make the control plane less clean than the UI suggests.

## Customer And Dealer Experience Review

- Customer/dealer payment experiences should clearly separate secure tokenized public payment links from internal staff/customer-account lookups. The current route layout still makes that boundary hard to audit.
- Dealer portal ownership and document/payment scoping are well documented and appear directionally strong, but next-step language remains coarse. Dealers need "what GND is waiting on" with manufacturing proof, not just preparing or ready.
- Customer trust improves when checkout links, quote acceptance, and payment verification are signed, short-lived, and narrow. The account-number pay portal still deserves a signed/customer-authenticated boundary.

## Door-Manufacturing Workflow Gaps

- Manufacturing readiness should speak in door terms: dimension, handing/swing, profile, material availability, production assignment, produced quantity, packed quantity, delivery/pickup status, and final proof.
- Dealer-facing order readiness should expose a compact readiness panel backed by the same production/material/dispatch authorities used internally.
- Packing should support "resume my count" the same way proof supports "resume my signature/photos."
- Public and internal payment paths should be documented and enforced as separate contracts so office staff can train reps without explaining exceptions.
