## Worker Usability Review

Sales and office workflows continue to improve, but the codebase still asks workers to infer too much from technical or generic states. Sales, production, warehouse, and dispatch teams need shared words for the same order phase: quote pending, payment ready, material blocked, production scheduled, packed, out for delivery, pickup ready, fulfilled, or exception. Today those ideas are spread across sales status, fulfillment status, dispatch status, inventory demand state, and dealer next-step state.

Mobile worker ergonomics remain a priority. The mobile app has recovery/diagnostic paths, but invoice creation still imports mock data and relies on fallback construction. For mixed-skill workers, the important question is not whether the form can technically hydrate; it is whether a driver, warehouse worker, or field salesperson can safely recover after app backgrounding, poor network, duplicate taps, or partial customer data.

Manager oversight is also uneven. Active Sessions looks like an admin control but is backed by local mock devices and fake logout behavior. That pattern erodes trust in genuine controls, especially for finance, payments, inventory, and dealership operations.

## Customer And Dealer Experience Review

Customer-facing payment and account lookup flows remain the highest customer-trust concern. Tokenized checkout and quote-acceptance routes are valid patterns, but broad public sales/payment/customer-account procedures make it difficult to explain the system boundary cleanly to staff or auditors. The customer pay portal should have a crisp signed-link or authenticated boundary, explicit expiry, and minimal data return for the exact task being performed.

Dealer experience is protected better than older reports: dealer portal routes use dealer-protected procedures and scope payment link/order actions to dealer ownership. The remaining gap is operational clarity. Dealers can see a friendly next step, but not enough manufacturing readiness detail to answer customer questions without contacting the office.

Document and email reliability remains tied to typecheck health. Quote, invoice, pickup, delivery, and payment emails are key customer touchpoints; a red email package typecheck should stay visible until it is clean.

## Door-Manufacturing Workflow Gaps

- Quote readiness still needs stronger visible checks for door size, swing, hinge side, profile, jamb/casing assumptions, hardware, glass/special-order dependencies, and customer approval state before production.
- Production readiness needs a common blocker model: missing payment, missing material, dimension/profile exception, pending manager override, supplier dependency, schedule conflict, and delivery/pickup constraint.
- Inventory allocation visibility remains the critical operational gap until the correctness cutover is release-clean.
- Dealer and customer surfaces need manufacturing-native progress rather than generic fulfillment phrasing.
- Mobile and dispatch surfaces need quick action patterns that map to floor work: scan, photo proof, packed, loaded, delivered, customer unavailable, damaged/missing item, return to shop, and manager escalation.
- Runtime logging should support accountability without dumping full customer, payment, notification, or sales payloads into generic logs.
