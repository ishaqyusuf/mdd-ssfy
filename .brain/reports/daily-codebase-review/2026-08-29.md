## Worker Usability Review

The strongest worker-facing improvements are in production and dispatch context. The dashboard now has active Brain coverage for production worker workspace modernization, Sales Overview production preflight, inventory-backed fulfillment, and dispatch packing simplification. Mobile dispatch details show warehouse/packing stats and not-ready alerts in `apps/mobile/src/features/dispatch/components/dispatch-detail-screen/components/scroll-content.tsx`, which is the right direction for mixed-skill teams.

The remaining usability gap is proof and action ergonomics. Warehouse staff, drivers, installers, and production workers should not have to interpret abstract statuses. The best next pattern is scan-first quick actions with large targets, visible retry/sync state, photo proof for exceptions, and a manager-readable activity trail. Brain already tracks most of this through the driver dashboard command center and inventory cutover plans, so the report should not create duplicate backlog work.

Office/admin usability is weaker around security and route semantics. A visible mock Active Sessions card and many public operational routes make it harder to train admins on what controls actually work.

## Customer And Dealer Experience Review

Dealer next-step guidance is materially better than a generic order-status label. It separates customer payment, GND/office payment, preparation, ready, fulfilled, and cancellation phases. That is useful for dealers.

The customer/dealer gap is manufacturing readiness clarity. Dealers and customers need fewer vague "preparing" states and more specific, non-internal explanations such as "waiting for payment", "confirming dimensions", "materials being allocated", "queued for production", "ready for pickup", "out for delivery", or "delivery issue needs attention". The completed Sales Overview production preflight can be reused as a manager/admin source of truth, but customer/dealer text should be simpler and less operationally noisy.

The public customer pay portal is the biggest trust concern. Account-number lookup may be convenient for office-assisted work, but customer-facing money workflows need scoped links, strict expiration, and clear auditability.

## Door-Manufacturing Workflow Gaps

- Quote readiness: The app has sales preflight coverage, but dealer/customer surfaces still do not visibly explain dimension, profile, hinge, swing, no-handle, tax, shipping, supplier/pricing, or PDF readiness blockers.
- Material allocation: Inventory work is improving but not release-clean. Sales, production, purchasing, and warehouse teams need a shared "can build now / awaiting inbound / needs purchase / exception" view before inventory-backed fulfillment can be treated as authoritative.
- Production readiness: Production status needs to be connected to preflight evidence, not only payment or coarse fulfillment status.
- Packing and delivery proof: Mobile dispatch has stronger context, but the remaining ideal is scan-first loading, barcode verification, photo proof for exceptions, and weak-network retry confidence.
- Payment trust: Square terminal and wallet flows need route-level boundaries that are as clear as the operational UI.
- Communication reliability: Email typecheck failure and notification payload logging both touch customer/dealer trust channels.

## Cross-Surface Consistency Gaps

- Route protection is not consistent with product sensitivity. Some sensitive actions are protected or query-guarded, while other payment, sales, supplier, customer, and filter paths remain public.

- Customer communication channels are not consistently release-clean: email typecheck fails, notification payload logging is too permissive, and customer pay portal access semantics are weak.
