## Worker Usability Review

The best worker-facing improvements are where the system uses one clear server operation with a visible state: mobile dispatch proof completion now does this, and production submission material review has a documented approval path. The weaker areas are where users can see or trigger operational state through generic/public routes, or where UI implies a working control that is not real, such as Active Sessions.

For mixed-skill sales, production, warehouse, and driver teams, the next usability gains should come from fewer ambiguous buttons, clear role ownership, and proof-first flows: "request approval", "approve", "allocate", "pick", "pack", "dispatch", "complete with proof", and "manager review" should each map to one explainable server action and one visible audit trail.

## Customer And Dealer Experience Review

Dealership quote-to-order has moved in the right direction: quotes lock after request decisions, direct conversion is retired, approval guidance is deterministic, and dealer/customer balances are separated. The remaining dealer opportunity is a proof-backed fulfillment timeline after approval so dealers do not need to call the office to understand payment, production, packing, pickup, or delivery status.

Customer payment remains the major trust gap. A phone/account keyed pay lookup that returns payment/wallet/terminal context is too broad for a customer-facing surface. Before expanding online-paid follow-up and receipt resend workflows, the payment boundary should be signed, short-lived, auditable, and field-minimized.

## Door-Manufacturing Workflow Gaps

Door manufacturing needs preflight checks that prevent expensive rework before an order reaches production: dimensions, swing, hinge side, profile, moulding, supplier price coverage, glass/hardware choices, customer/dealer pricing, inventory allocation, and production readiness should be visible before approval/release.

The codebase has many of the domain pieces: door/HPT size and swing handling in `packages/sales/src/sales-form`, supplier variant pricing in `packages/inventory`, production preflight in Sales Overview, and inventory-backed fulfillment plans. The gap is a consistent release gate that joins those checks into a simple manager/operator view.

Inventory-backed fulfillment remains the main manufacturing constraint. Brain still records unresolved drift and stopped repairs, so allocation/projection outputs should be treated as useful but not fully authoritative until read-only release evidence is clean.
