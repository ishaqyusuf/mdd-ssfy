## Worker Usability Review

Sales and office users have many workflows that are close to trainable: Sales Overview, payment summaries, paid-order handoff, and production submission review are all actively improving. The weak point is boundary clarity. If a worker can create steps, restore orders, resolve payments, or edit suppliers through broadly public procedures, the UI may feel fine while the accountability model is fragile.

Production staff need "ready/not ready" reasons that match their daily language: dimensions complete, swing/hinge/profile selected, payment state, customer approval, material allocation, hardware availability, frame/door slab readiness, packed status, and delivery or pickup assignment. Current work on production views and item single-view helps, but dealer and customer-facing status still does not appear to expose the same manufacturing readiness model.

Warehouse and dispatch workflows are progressing through guarded approval, dispatch lifecycle work, mobile delivery hardening, and packing parity. The remaining usability gap is scan/photo-first proof for mixed-skill workers. Forms that require reading dense records or selecting from broad lists will be slower and more error-prone than barcode, photo, signature, and quick-confirm flows.

## Customer And Dealer Experience Review

Customer payment convenience is important, but the current public pay-portal shape appears too broad for the data returned. Customers should see only the invoices and payment actions they are entitled to see through a tokenized or otherwise constrained access path.

Dealer next-step guidance is tracked and partly implemented, but dealers still need more practical manufacturing transparency: what is waiting on them, what is waiting on GND, what is material-ready, what is in production, what is packed, and what is ready for pickup or delivery.

Documents and print/PDF reliability remain important to trust. Because typecheck is red, changes around email, payment receipts, invoices, and dealer/customer documents should be treated cautiously until workspace typecheck is green again.

## Door-Manufacturing Workflow Gaps

- Quote/order readiness should explicitly validate dimension, swing, hinge, profile, material, finish, hardware, customer approval, and payment readiness before production release.
- Inventory allocation should distinguish estimated availability from reserved, picked, packed, consumed, returned, or adjusted stock.
- Dealer-facing status should mirror internal manufacturing states instead of relying on text-derived `preparing`, `ready`, and `completed` buckets.
- Mobile warehouse/driver flows should bias toward barcode scan, photo proof, signature capture, and short exception reasons.
- Customer communications should distinguish paid, awaiting approval, material-ready, in production, packed, out for delivery, ready for pickup, delivered, and completed.
