## Worker Usability Review

Production, inventory, dispatch, and jobs users now have more table-standardized surfaces and several mutation boundaries are stronger than in earlier reports. The remaining usability risk is trust: if a worker sees mock active sessions, noisy debug output, or public routes that can start task work without an obvious permission boundary, training becomes harder because the UI and system accountability do not line up.

Mobile worker ergonomics remain mixed. The mobile sales form parity and save-diagnostics plans are correctly scoped, but current source still has job-form debug logs and older hooks that reset form state on sheet close. For mixed-skill installers and warehouse workers, the high-value next step is not more controls; it is fewer ambiguous save/failure states, clear retry copy, and one obvious "what happened" record after submitting a job, invoice, delivery proof, or packing proof.

## Customer And Dealer Experience Review

Dealer experience is materially improved by completed next-step guidance and quote edit locks. The remaining dealer-side release risk is dealership expansion itself: branding, customer privacy, direct-ship snapshots, secure applications, and lifecycle controls are implemented but still gated by schema application and browser proof in `.brain/tasks/in-progress.md`.

Customer payment experience still needs a sharper trust model. The public pay portal can resolve by customer id or phone-like account input and returns pending order, contact, wallet, terminal, and payment-method context. Customer self-service, wallet visibility, and quick account creation are already part of the Sales Payment V2 direction, but the current public portal route should be handled as a risk until it is tokenized, session-scoped, or deliberately constrained.
