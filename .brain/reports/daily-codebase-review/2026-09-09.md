## Highest-Risk Findings

### P1 - Legacy sales write paths still use public tRPC wrappers

Evidence: `apps/api/src/trpc/routers/sales.route.ts` keeps operational writes under `publicProcedure`, including `salesPayWithWallet`, `startNewSales`, `resolvePayment`, `restore`, `saveSupplier`, `deleteSupplier`, and `updateStepMeta`.

Why it matters: wallet payment, payment resolution, restoring deleted sales, supplier management, and workflow metadata changes affect revenue, fulfillment, pricing setup, and audit accountability. Even if inner query helpers check some context, mixed-skill office users and future agents need the route boundary to communicate the trust model clearly.

### P1 - Square terminal side effects remain public

Evidence: `apps/api/src/trpc/routers/checkout.route.ts` exposes `generateDeviceCode` as `publicProcedure`, and `apps/api/src/trpc/routers/square-test.route.ts` exposes a `test` mutation that calls `squareClient.terminal.checkouts.create` with a caller-provided device id.

Why it matters: terminal device-code generation and checkout creation are external payment-provider side effects. They should be staff-authenticated, permission-shaped, environment-gated, or retired from the public app router.

### P1 - Broad typecheck remains red in a shared package boundary

Evidence: `bun run typecheck` fails in `@gnd/settings` because `packages/errors/src/index.ts` is not NodeNext-compatible for a consumer that requires explicit relative `.js` extensions.

Why it matters: this blocks broad validation and makes it harder to tell whether current production receipt, Sales Orders, and payment changes introduced new cross-package regressions.

### P2 - Task-event controls still expose public wrappers

Evidence: `apps/api/src/trpc/routers/task-events.route.ts` imports only `publicProcedure`, then exposes list/get/update/history/runNow/runTest/runStatus wrappers.

Why it matters: Brain says task monitor simplification and diagnostics are implemented, and previous query-layer work may contain inner Super Admin checks. The public wrapper still weakens reviewability and makes task-control routes look callable without a session at the API boundary.

### P2 - Production receipt follow-up is promising but not fully accepted

Evidence: `apps/api/src/trpc/routers/sales.route.ts` now uses `protectedProcedure` for `productionPendingInbounds`, `receiveProductionInbound`, and `cancelProductionInbound`. `packages/sales/src/production-inbound.ts` scopes worker receipt by assignment, expected revision, policy, receipt audit, and transaction locks. `packages/sales/src/production-inbound-cancel.ts` restricts cancellation to inbound admins and verifies audit evidence before compensation. Brain still records worker browser acceptance as blocked by the active account redirecting to `/sales-rep`.

Why it matters: this is a high-value direction for production workers, but the worker-facing path cannot be called release-proven until role/session acceptance is completed. For a mixed-skill production team, the difference between "materials received" and "contact supervisor" has to be verified in the actual worker surface.

### P2 - Settings Active Sessions still shows mock security state

Evidence: `apps/dashboard/src/components/user-logged-in-devices.tsx` renders `mockDevices`, waits 500ms on logout, and has a TODO for real logout logic.

Why it matters: account/session visibility is a trust feature for office/admin users. Showing fake location/IP/device rows can train users to ignore an important security surface.

### P2 - Dealer fulfillment guidance still lacks manufacturing readiness detail

Evidence: `apps/dealership/src/lib/dealer-next-step.ts` can distinguish request review, GND payment, ready for pickup/delivery, and completed fulfillment, but the post-payment default is generic "GND is preparing your pickup/delivery." It does not expose production/material/document blockers such as missing dimensions, swing, profile, inventory allocation, or manufacturing review.

Why it matters: dealers need simple next steps, but they also need enough specificity to answer their own customers without calling the office for every blocked order.

### P2 - Mobile dispatch completion and packing still carry restart-sensitive gaps

Evidence: `apps/mobile/src/features/dispatch/components/dispatch-detail-screen/index.tsx` preserves proof only while the completion screen remains mounted after a failed request, while the mobile invoice form has MMKV recovery in `apps/mobile/src/features/sales/invoice-form/lib/local-recovery.ts`. Brain already documents mobile delivery hardening and proof restart recovery as a known issue.

Why it matters: drivers and warehouse staff work in weak-network and interruption-prone conditions. Packing/proof flows need the same durable recovery discipline now present in the mobile invoice form.

## Worker Usability Review

Production worker receipt is moving in the right direction: workers can receive only assigned inbound material when the policy allows it, cannot use the general Inventory receive route, and cannot cancel a receipt. The current UI direction also suppresses admin-grade secondary alerts from worker views, which fits mixed technical comfort levels.

Remaining usability risk is acceptance, not intent. Brain states the current browser account redirects from `/production/dashboard` to `/sales-rep`, so the worker role has not been validated in the real surface. That means the "Yes, received", "Materials received", "Waiting for material confirmation", and "Contact your supervisor" journey is still not proven in the exact role that will use it.

Mobile has stronger invoice draft recovery than dispatch packing/proof. For worker ergonomics, dispatch needs comparable durable local recovery, explicit stale-state prompts, and server-owned action blockers before field rollout.

## Customer And Dealer Experience Review

Customer-facing checkout and quote acceptance are richer than earlier reports, but public checkout/device/payment routes are still mixed with staff side effects. Tokenized public payment is legitimate; staff terminal setup, Square device-code creation, and test terminal checkouts should not share that public trust boundary.

Dealer guidance has improved payment and fulfillment language, including separate GND/customer balances. The gap is manufacturing transparency: dealers see "preparing" without a plain-language reason when production, material, document, dimension, hinge/swing, profile, or readiness evidence blocks the order.

## Door-Manufacturing Workflow Gaps

- Production receipt has audit and cancellation direction, but live worker acceptance remains blocked.
- Dealer views do not expose a compact manufacturing readiness panel for material, dimensions, swing/handing, profile, production status, packing, pickup/delivery, and documents.
- Mobile dispatch proof has good server-side concepts, but the active mobile completion flow is still weaker than the invoice form's restart-safe local recovery.
- Public Square/device routes do not align with the operational reality that terminal pairing and checkout creation are staff-only payment-room actions.
- Account security still cannot show real active sessions or revoke another device, which matters for shared office/shop machines.
