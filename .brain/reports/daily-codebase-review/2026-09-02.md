## Highest-Risk Findings

P1 - Public Square test checkout remains mounted and can create Terminal checkout side effects.

Evidence: `apps/api/src/trpc/routers/square-test.route.ts:1` imports `publicProcedure`; `apps/api/src/trpc/routers/square-test.route.ts:5` exposes `test`; `apps/api/src/trpc/routers/square-test.route.ts:16` calls `squareClient.terminal.checkouts.create` for a fixed `BigInt(1000)` amount; `apps/api/src/trpc/routers/_app.ts:97` mounts `squareTest`.

Why it matters: this bypasses the safer staff terminal-payment path documented elsewhere in Brain and can prompt real devices outside normal staff authorization. In a counter or warehouse setting, accidental terminal checkouts directly damage trust in payment tooling.

P1 - Public task-event scheduler controls remain available for list/update/run/test/status operations.

Evidence: `apps/api/src/trpc/routers/task-events.route.ts:10` imports `publicProcedure`; `apps/api/src/trpc/routers/task-events.route.ts:18` creates the router; `list`, `get`, `update`, `history`, `runNow`, `runTest`, and `runStatus` are public at lines 21, 26, 31, 42, 53, 58, and 67; `apps/api/src/trpc/routers/_app.ts:100` mounts `taskEvents`.

Why it matters: the scheduler control plane influences background automation. Even if downstream handlers validate some work, anonymous or wrong-context access should not be able to inspect or run operational tasks.

P1 - Public customer/pay/checkout surfaces still mix signed customer flows with account lookup, device, and settlement actions.

Evidence: `apps/api/src/trpc/routers/customer.route.ts:58` exposes `getCustomerPayPortal` publicly; `apps/api/src/db/queries/customer.ts:2064` accepts only `accountNo`, then reads pending sales and wallet state at lines 2077-2079. `apps/api/src/trpc/routers/checkout.route.ts:18-43` exposes checkout initialization, quote acceptance, payment-link creation, payment verification, and device-code generation as public procedures. `apps/api/src/db/queries/checkout.ts:925-1015` verifies payments by supplied identifiers, and `apps/api/src/db/queries/checkout.ts:1307-1327` creates Square device codes.

Why it matters: tokenized customer checkout can be intentionally public, but it needs a crisp signed-token boundary. The current public grouping makes it harder to prove that account-number lookup, wallet exposure, terminal device setup, and payment settlement are available only to the intended actor.

P2 - Public organization create/list remains inconsistent with office and future tenant scoping.

Evidence: `apps/api/src/db/queries/organization.ts:1` imports `publicProcedure`; `getOrganizationProfile` reads all organizations at line 9; `createOrganizationProfile` is public at line 27 and creates an organization at lines 34-41; `apps/api/src/trpc/routers/organization.route.ts:7-9` exposes both through `orgs`.

Why it matters: Brain treats Organization as office/location authority and separates it from future SaaS Tenant authority. Public organization writes conflict with office-scoped accountability, fulfillment-office assignment, and multi-location auditability.

P2 - Broad public filter metadata remains available for operational surfaces.

Evidence: `apps/api/src/trpc/routers/filters.route.ts:34-123` exposes customer, customer service, dispatch, employee, inbound, inventory, job, notification, product report, sales resolution, backlog, sales production, order, quote, accounting, project unit, and unit production filter metadata mostly through `publicProcedure`; only contractor payout is protected in this router.

Why it matters: filter endpoints often leak real operational names, employees, customers, jobs, inventory categories, and work states. Even when the payload is metadata, it can expose production volume, customer activity, warehouse state, and sales pipeline shape.

P2 - Monorepo typecheck remains red at a shared package boundary.

Evidence: `packages/errors/package.json:5` declares ESM; `packages/errors/src/index.ts:1-21` uses extensionless relative exports; `packages/settings/tsconfig.json:5-12` opts into NodeNext. The broad `bun run typecheck` stops in `@gnd/settings`.

Why it matters: this blocks broad validation of revenue, dispatch, dealership, mobile, and inventory changes. It also keeps future work from distinguishing new failures from baseline noise.

P2 - Mobile packing quantity drafts are volatile while proof drafts are restart-safe.

Evidence: `apps/mobile/src/features/dispatch/components/dispatch-packing-form.tsx:32-36` stores packing quantities, LH/RH counts, notes, and errors in React state, and submit/reset paths clear that state at lines 57-73 and 99-119. In contrast, `apps/mobile/src/features/dispatch/hooks/use-dispatch-proof-draft.ts:25-224` and `apps/mobile/src/features/dispatch/lib/dispatch-proof-draft-storage.ts:1-201` persist proof drafts through AsyncStorage and app-owned files.

Why it matters: warehouse workers can recover signature/photo proof after interruption, but count-entry work can still disappear before submission. For door counts, LH/RH quantities, and packed-item checks under weak network conditions, losing partial count work causes rework and mistakes.

P2 - Dealer next-step guidance remains payment/fulfillment coarse rather than manufacturing-readiness specific.

Evidence: `apps/dealership/src/lib/dealer-next-step.ts:107-178` derives next steps from GND balance, customer balance, delivery option, order status, and bounded fulfillment status. `apps/dealership/src/components/dealer-portal/dealer-order-overview.tsx:53-89` displays progress around order approved, customer payment, GND payment, and fulfillment. It does not expose dimensions, swing/handing, material blockers, production readiness, packing proof, or ETA evidence.

Why it matters: dealer users can see preparing or ready, but not why. Door operations need clarity around what is waiting on pricing review, material allocation, production, packing, pickup, delivery scheduling, or customer balance.

P3 - Active Sessions remains a mock UI while auth/session metadata exists elsewhere.

Evidence: `apps/dashboard/src/components/user-logged-in-devices.tsx:9-24` defines hard-coded `mockDevices`; `apps/dashboard/src/components/user-logged-in-devices.tsx:35-39` keeps logout as TODO; `apps/dashboard/src/components/user-logged-in-devices.tsx:47-51` renders mock data in the profile settings table.

Why it matters: account security and device accountability matter for office/admin staff, dealers, and shared terminals. A mock session list teaches users that session controls exist when they do not actually revoke devices.

## Worker Usability Review

- Sales reps benefit from recent document-readiness and status-only completion work, but the route boundary still makes it hard to explain which money/customer actions are staff-only versus signed customer-token actions.
- Production workers now have stronger status and submission flow coverage in Brain, but the shared typecheck baseline keeps release confidence lower than it should be for production-payroll, material-review, and fulfillment changes.
- Warehouse and driver mobile work is materially better than older reviews: proof drafts are recoverable and dispatch packing now uses protected dispatch mutations. The remaining usability risk is interruption recovery for pre-submit packing quantities.
- Managers still need a cleaner operational control plane. Public task-event controls and public filter metadata make the backend boundary less strict than the staff UI implies.

## Customer And Dealer Experience Review

- Customer/dealer payment experiences should clearly separate signed public payment links from internal staff/customer-account lookups. The current public route layout still makes that boundary hard to audit.
- Dealer portal ownership and document/payment scoping are directionally strong, and dealer print now checks sales document readiness. The next gap is explanatory: dealers need to know what GND is waiting on in manufacturing terms.
- Customer trust improves when checkout links, quote acceptance, and payment verification are signed, short-lived, and narrow. The account-number pay portal still deserves a signed/customer-authenticated boundary before deeper self-service expansion.

## Door-Manufacturing Workflow Gaps

- Manufacturing readiness should speak in door terms: dimensions, handing/swing, profile, material availability, production assignment, produced quantity, packed quantity, delivery/pickup status, and final proof.
- Dealer-facing order readiness should expose a compact readiness panel backed by the same production, material, document, and dispatch authorities used internally.
- Packing should support "resume my count" the same way proof supports "resume my signature/photos."
- Inventory correctness remains operationally important, but repair dry-runs/applies are intentionally stopped by user request and should not resume inside this automation.
