# Define The Material Handoff Action

Type: grilling
Status: open
Blocked by: None
Parent: [`../map.md`](../map.md)

## Question

What exact server-derived evidence opens and resolves a Material Handoff Action?
Define tracked and applicable demand, partial supplier coverage, active versus
terminal inbound shipment states, prompt-only `ORDERED` evidence, supplier-less
or legacy inbounds, split suppliers, partially received demand, cancelled
demand, non-stock and not-applicable rows, stale projections, and concurrent
changes.

The answer must remove the action when a valid supplier commitment is recorded
without pretending that ordered material has physically arrived.

## Comments

> *This was generated by AI during Wayfinder issue commenting.*

**Proposed answer:** Open a Material Handoff Action when a payment-qualified
order has positive, applicable tracked demand whose remaining quantity is not
fulfilled and is not covered by an active linked inbound shipment.

- Active pending, ordered/in-progress, and partially received inbounds cover
  their linked outstanding quantities.
- Partial coverage keeps the action open until every applicable remaining
  quantity is covered.
- Terminal, cancelled, deleted, or unrelated inbounds do not count.
- A prompt-only `ORDERED` status does not prove that the supplier order was
  represented in inventory.
- A canonical active inbound may count even when its supplier is missing for
  legacy compatibility, but it must be linked to the demand.
- Non-stock, untracked, not-applicable, zero-required, cancelled, and fulfilled
  demand is excluded.
- Projection failure returns a separate unavailable state; the client must not
  invent a Material action.
- The server re-evaluates evidence after every relevant write before opening or
  clearing the action.

**Why:** This makes the pill mean “the purchasing handoff is missing,” not “the
material has not arrived.”

**Assumptions / risk:** Supplier-less legacy inbounds remain valid coverage only
when linked through canonical demand ownership.
